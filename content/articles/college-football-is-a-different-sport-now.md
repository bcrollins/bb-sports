> Disclosure: I’m a Florida Gators fan and I’m a college student at UF, so I am as inside this as a non-roster person can be. I am also a journalism major, which means I get yelled at in two directions on this topic — by people my parents’ age who think NIL ruined the sport, and by people my age who think every "this isn’t real college football anymore" column is gatekeeping. So let’s do this column the way I want every BB Sports college football column to read: tell you what I think, tell you why, tell you what the strongest case against me is.

## The headline

The version of college football most of you grew up with is gone. **It is not coming back.** Pretending otherwise is the laziest column in the genre. NIL is permanent. The portal is permanent. The expanded playoff is permanent. Conferences will keep moving. The TV deals will keep rewriting things. There is no "back." There is only what the sport actually is now.

What it is now: a sport where **the best players are paid, the players who don’t fit can leave, and twelve teams instead of four get to play for the championship.**

I’m sorry — when did this become the bad version?

## What we got

**Players get paid.** The thing the NCAA spent thirty years lying about — that "amateurism" was a moral position rather than an accounting trick — got dragged into the sunlight. Top recruits are no longer being asked to generate hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue for free. Whatever you think about how the money flows, the floor of "athletes get nothing while the coach makes ten million" is gone. **That floor was indefensible.** Whatever the new floor looks like, it is on the right side of indefensible.

**Players can leave.** A coach can no longer recruit you, sit you on the bench for two seasons because depth chart politics, and threaten your scholarship if you ask to transfer. That is — let me check my notes here — *good*. That is unambiguously good for nineteen-year-olds. If you’d told me ten years ago that an offensive lineman could go from a school where he was the seventh tackle to a school where he gets to start as a fifth-year senior, I’d have called it a fantasy. It’s reality. **It is also why the sport has more good football than it’s ever had.**

**Twelve teams instead of four.** Yes, the regular season matters slightly less. Yes, a one-loss SEC team will sometimes look like a two-loss team that backed in. **And we get six more weeks of bracket football a year.** I’m not pretending I think that math is bad.

## What we lost (the honest part of this column)

I owe you the other side of this if I’m going to call it honest.

**Team building takes a hit.** The roster you saw on August 28 is not the roster you’ll see in November. Continuity coaching — the slow build of a program over four years of one freshman class — is harder to do. There’s a thing about senior leadership that I think the new system actually does undercut, and the column that says "the senior captain who chose to come back" is real. Those columns are not all gatekeeping. Some of them are watching something specific go away.

**The tampering layer is ugly.** Boosters texting kids on the recruiting visit of a school they have no business near is a real problem. The rules around it are unenforceable. Until the sport gets a real cap and a real agent infrastructure that the NCAA can actually regulate without a federal lawsuit per case, this is going to be a mess. **You can tell me this is bad and I will not argue.**

**The one-time-only transfer rule is gone in a way that matters.** A kid who transfers three times by his sophomore year — that’s not a thriving developmental environment for him *or* for the program. There’s a real conversation here about academic stability, mental health, and the difference between freedom and chaos. I don’t want to write the chaos column. But I want to acknowledge somebody could.

## Where I actually land

College football is a different sport now and I think it is a **better one for fans, a better one for top-tier players, and a more chaotic one for coaches and athletic directors.** That’s a tradeoff I can live with. I am not nostalgic for a sport where the people doing the work didn’t get paid.

Tell me where I’m wrong. The comments are open. **Be specific.**

— **Brad**
