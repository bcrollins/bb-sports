The Lakers are not a real team.

I'm not editorializing. I'm reading the depth chart.

LeBron James is forty years old and is **still** the best player on this roster. That sentence is supposed to be a celebration. It is actually an indictment. The Lakers' front office has had a literal decade to build a roster around the most marketable player in sports and the answer they came up with this year is **the rest of the league's leftovers and a couple of guys on minimum deals**.

Look at the rotation. Six of the top eight players were unsigned by their previous teams. Two of them are explicitly there to mop up garbage time. Their starting power forward was a Summer League ringer two years ago. **Their bench point guard was on the open market in February.** They are paying the second-highest tax bill in the league for that roster.

The defense is 22nd in the league. The half-court offense without LeBron on the floor is 28th. The transition defense is 30th. **They have not won a playoff series since Anthony Davis was healthy and Father Time was kinder.** Stop pretending the brand is making them a contender. The brand is the only thing keeping the cameras pointed at them.

I have nothing against the Lakers. I have everything against pretending the Lakers are a real team because the jerseys are pretty. **They are not a top-10 team in the league.** They are a top-10 *story* in the league. Those are not the same thing and I'm tired of writers using "Lakers" as a synonym for "contender" because it gets clicks.

Drop them on the rankings. The case for dropping them is that the roster has zero answers to anybody serious in May. The case against dropping them is "but LeBron." **LeBron is forty.** That case got cooked the day he started losing seven inches of vertical to gravity.

If you want a real top-10 team in basketball today, you have a list. **The Lakers are not on it.** I'm done pretending.

— **Brad**
