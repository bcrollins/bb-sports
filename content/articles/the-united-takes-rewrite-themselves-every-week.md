> Disclosure: I’m a United fan. Have been for years. The takes you’re about to read I have texted into a group chat at 2 a.m. — I’m just publishing them this time.

There’s a Manchester United column I have read approximately eight hundred times in my life. The headline changes. The structure does not. Three weeks ago, after the away win at City, the column was *"They’re back. The project is real. Champions League knockout football is on the table."* Two weeks ago, after the home loss to Brighton, the column was *"This club is structurally broken. The whole thing needs to be torn down. Hire a new sporting director. Sell six players."* This past weekend, after the win at Liverpool, the column was once again *"They’re back."*

**It’s the same column. The verbs change.** The column is wrong every time, by the way. The takes were wrong on the way up. They were wrong on the way down. They’ll be wrong this week. The reason they’re wrong is they’re writing about a one-week sample of a sport that punishes one-week samples.

## The actual thing that’s true

The actual thing that’s true is that this team is **mid-table good with one or two performances per season that look like a Champions League team and one or two that look like a Championship team.** That’s the band. The band is wide. The band moves with injuries, the schedule, and which goalkeeper is on the team sheet that week.

If you want me to be more specific: the goal difference per match versus the top six is fine. The goal difference per match versus the bottom six is *also* fine — but only when the starters are healthy. The drop-off from starters to depth is the steepest in the top six. **That’s the whole story.** Every dramatic column in the cycle is some version of "the depth chart is thin and we are panicking about it after the third game on a six-day rest."

## Why we keep doing this anyway

I’ll tell you why I keep writing this column even though I just told you the column is wrong: **I love arguing about it.** That’s the actual sport for fans. The "is the project real?" debate doesn’t get won — it gets had. Forever. With my friends. In the group chat. At three in the morning. With increasingly bad takes that we will defend the next week.

**That’s fan content.** The way most major sites write about United has scrubbed all of that out. It pretends every match is a referendum. Every loss requires a 1,200-word eulogy. Every win requires a 1,200-word coronation. None of that sounds like the way I, or anybody I know, actually talks about this team.

So here’s the BB Sports rule on Manchester United, going forward:

1. **A single match never gets to be a referendum.** Not a win, not a loss. Three games or it didn’t happen.
2. **The cycle is the bit.** When the takes start rewriting themselves, I will note — in the column — that the takes are rewriting themselves.
3. **The "tear it down" column will not appear on this site after a single home loss.** Ever.

That’s the deal. That’s what I think the right way to write about this team is. Yell at me.

— **Brad**
