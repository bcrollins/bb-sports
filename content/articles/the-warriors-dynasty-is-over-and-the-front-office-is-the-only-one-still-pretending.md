The Warriors dynasty is over. **Call it.**

Steph Curry is still the most dangerous shooter alive. He is also forty. Klay is on a different team. Draymond is on his last contract and looks every minute of his thirty-five years on a basketball court. The roster around the last legend in the building has been a *play-in team for two straight years* and the front office is still drafting and signing like it's 2017.

Look at the depth chart. Six of the eight rotation players are guys who got cut somewhere else. The starting five has a positive net rating of *single digits*. The second unit hasn't been net-positive in two years. **The franchise that broke basketball with the gravity of three Hall of Famers on the floor at once is now a play-in team that nobody outside of San Francisco is afraid of.**

The case for "well they still made the playoffs" is the case for managed decline. The case for "Curry can do it one more time" requires the front office to actually build a roster that lets him. They haven't. They've signed a bunch of veteran minimums and called it a depth chart. That isn't building. That's *the laziest version of a retool that has ever been dressed up as a contender plan*.

Drop them. The Warriors had two slots of grace in my preseason because of who they used to be. They're a sub-.500 team in May. They are not in the top 10. They are barely in the top 15. **The hat is doing all the lifting and the hat doesn't suit up.**

Curry deserves better. The front office isn't going to give it to him.

— **Brad**
