I’m Brad Benson. I’m a junior at the University of Florida, journalism and sports media major, class of 2027. I’m from Boca Raton. I root, openly, for the **Bears, the Panthers, Manchester United, the Florida Gators, the Bulls, and the Cubs.** Now you know my biases. They aren’t going anywhere, and pretending otherwise would be the first lie I told you on this site.

Here’s what I’m trying to do.

## The fan view is the whole point

There’s a way most sports websites are written, and there’s the way fans actually talk about sports. Those are not the same thing. The way most websites are written, every paragraph hedges. Every take is balanced down to nothing. Every column ends with a polite "we’ll see how it plays out." That’s fine. It’s also boring, and it doesn’t sound like a single human I’ve ever watched a game with.

The way fans actually talk about sports? They take a side. They argue. They get loud. They yell at the TV. They text the group chat in all caps. They have *receipts*. **That’s** the register I want this site in.

Pat McAfee figured this out for talk radio. I want to figure it out for written sports journalism.

## Three rules of the house

**1. Bias is disclosed, not hidden.** I just told you which teams I root for. When I write a Bears column, you’ll see that disclosure right above the byline. When I write about a team I have no stake in, that gets disclosed too. The point is you should never have to guess where the writer is coming from.

**2. Sources are cited inline.** When I tell you a stat, you’ll see where it came from. When I quote someone, the link to the press conference, the tweet, the interview — it’s right there. If a stat is mine and I’m eyeballing it, I’ll say that too.

**3. Corrections are public.** I’m a one-person operation right now. I’m going to get something wrong. When I do, the correction goes in the [corrections log](/corrections), with the date, what was wrong, and what it should have said. Not buried. Not silently edited.

## What about the AI?

The AI on this site is real and it’s loud about itself. Anything it touched carries a label. AI-assisted draft, edited by Brad Benson — that’s the tag. The AI does the things that would otherwise drown a one-person shop:

- Pulls breaking news from verified accounts and surfaces it for me to react to.
- Transcribes voice memos into rough drafts so I can talk a column out instead of typing it from cold.
- Drafts injury notes, power-rankings tables, and 300-word reactions to news I’d otherwise miss.
- Generates a counterpoint sidebar that pushes back on my take so the column has to survive an argument before it ships.
- Lays out the social posts and quote graphics for me.

What the AI **does not** do:

- It does not publish without my approval. Every AI-touched piece has a human approval gate. Always.
- It does not mimic my voice. I don’t want a fake Brad. The AI stays neutral; the take is mine.
- It does not write the **Bradley’s Take** sections. Those are mine. Always.

## What you should expect

Coverage, in roughly this order: **NFL, NHL, college football, soccer, NBA, MMA.** Lots of college early — the season is right there and college sports change fast enough that there’s always something to argue about.

You’ll get long-form opinion, breaking-news reactions, the occasional interview, podcast episodes when I get the rig set up, and short video clips for game reactions. No paywalls. Donations welcome but optional. You don’t have to pay to read the takes — that’s the whole point.

## The bar

If a hiring manager at ESPN landed on this site tomorrow, I want them to walk away thinking *this could be the next big thing*. Not because the writing is the most polished on the internet — it won’t be on day one — but because somebody actually decided to write the way fans actually talk, with receipts.

That’s the bet.

Welcome to BB Sports.

— **Brad**
