> Disclosure right up top: I’m a Bears fan. I have been my entire life. I will tell you why I think this is the best chance the franchise has had in decades, and I will tell you the exact games on the schedule that scare me. The job is not to talk you into it. The job is to lay the case out and let you yell at me in the comments.

## The case, in one paragraph

The skill group around the quarterback is, finally, real. The offensive line is the best it’s been in a decade. The defense, which has been the floor of every recent Bears team that flirted with respectability, has not regressed. The coaching staff, which is the variable I’d normally be most worried about, has actually shown a willingness to adjust week-to-week instead of waiting until the bye. **None of that has been true at the same time at any point I can remember.**

That’s the case. That’s the whole case. Now let’s talk about the holes.

## What I’m actually worried about

**The schedule.** The first six weeks have a Detroit/Green Bay double, a Minnesota road, and a non-conference trap game I won’t pretend I love. If the Bears come out 3-3, I’m not running for the cliff — but the noise will be louder than the team is good, and the noise is going to push some bad decisions if the staff isn’t careful.

**The injury map at receiver.** I am not going to count chickens here. The depth chart is one ankle away from the same conversation we’ve had every year. Nobody on a podcast can tell you whose ankle is going to roll. It’s the schedule the Bears didn’t pick — it’s also the schedule everybody plays.

**Red zone.** I keep coming back to red zone. The teams that win 11+ games convert in the red zone. The teams that win 8 don’t. I don’t need to see the analytics dashboard to know the Bears have lived on the wrong side of that line for a long time. Until I see them punch in three out of four against a real defense, I’m not declaring anything.

## What the homers will not tell you

If I’m being honest with the bias I told you about in the first paragraph: **most Bears columns this summer will be one of two things.** They’ll be 1,200 words of "this is the year" without a single hard question, or they’ll be 1,200 words of "same old Bears" without a single fair credit. Neither of those columns is good journalism and neither of those columns is even good fan content. The actual question is *what would have to be true for this team to win 11 games?* and *what would have to be true for them to crater?*

Eleven wins requires: red zone gets fixed, the line stays healthy through Thanksgiving, the secondary stays in coverage on third-and-long, and the staff doesn’t panic in a 1-3 stretch (which, again — this schedule has one in it).

Cratering requires: any one of the first three above goes sideways, plus the staff overreacts, plus the QB room flinches in a primetime game. That’s not nothing.

## The bet

I think this team wins 10. I think they’re 11 in a fair universe and 9 in a cruel one. I think they win a playoff game for the first time since I was old enough to remember it, and I think the conversation about this franchise gets dragged out of the basement it’s been in.

I will eat every word of this if I’m wrong. I’ll write the eat-my-words column myself, and I won’t bury it on the back page.

That’s the bet. **Yell at me in the comments.**

— **Brad**
