> Disclosure: I’m not a Wild fan. I’m not an Avs fan. I just love watching hockey and I love arguing about it more.

Final score: **Colorado 9, Minnesota 6.** Game 1 of the first round. Fifteen total goals in 60 minutes of regulation. The kind of scoreboard that, if you saw it on the bottom line and you blinked, you’d swear it was preseason.

It was not preseason. It was a playoff hockey game. And it was, depending on the angle you take, either the most entertaining game of the first round or the worst-defended one. **Both are right.** Don’t let anyone tell you the entertainment makes the bad hockey good.

## The "great game" angle is real

Look — fifteen goals. End-to-end action. Both benches were standing for a third of the game. The crowd was in it from puck drop. There were three lead changes before the second intermission. Every shift mattered. Every line had a chance.

If you’re a casual fan, this is the game you remember. This is the game that sucks somebody’s younger brother into hockey for the rest of his life. **Game 1 of the playoffs that ends 9-6 is a marketing department’s dream and a fan’s gift.** I’m not pretending I didn’t enjoy it.

## The "what the hell" angle is also real

Here’s the other thing that’s true. **A playoff team should not give up nine goals in a Game 1.** A playoff team should also not give up six. The defense in front of both nets was, charitably, rough. Uncharitably: the goaltending was non-existent. Not "had a bad night" non-existent. *Stand-and-watch* non-existent on at least four of those goals between the two of them.

You can have the most entertaining playoff opener of the decade and have it be **a complete failure of defensive structure** on both sides. Those statements are not in conflict. The "fans got their money’s worth" reading is true. The "this would not survive a third round" reading is also true.

## What I keep getting stuck on

The thing I keep getting stuck on is this: in the regular season, every coach in the league preaches *playoff hockey is different*. Tighter checks. More structure. Goaltending stands on its head. 2-1 games. 3-2 games. Block shots. Win the fifty-fifty pucks. **That’s the whole sermon for 82 games.**

And then the playoffs start and Game 1 ends 9-6.

If both teams give up six the next game, somebody’s coach is asleep. If both teams give up six in **all four games of this series**, somebody’s GM is making a phone call in June.

So yeah — I’ll watch every minute. I’ll be on the couch for Game 2. I’ll probably eat my words if both goalies stand on their heads tomorrow night. And I’ll still tell you what I told you tonight: **the entertainment doesn’t make it good hockey.** Two things can be true.

That’s the whole point of this site.
