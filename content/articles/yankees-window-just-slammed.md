I'm done.

The Yankees are not a top-3 team. They might not be a top-10 team. I have been talked into the hat for too long and I am pulling it off this column.

Judge is a top-2 hitter on the planet. Soto is a top-5 hitter on the planet. Together they are the most expensive corner-outfield duo in the history of professional sports. And the team around them, this season, looks like a Triple-A roster that took a wrong turn at the George Washington Bridge.

Let me be specific.

The infield defense is the worst it has been in twenty years. The rotation depth chart past the front two is a Designated for Assignment waiting list. The bullpen leverage situation is a coin flip from the sixth inning on. The bench is full of guys nobody at any other organization wanted. The manager keeps writing the same lineup that doesn't work. The front office keeps signing the same player profile that doesn't work. **This is not a top-3 roster. This isn't even close.**

You want to argue payroll? Fine. They are top-3 in payroll. Top-3 in payroll, top-15 in baseball. **That is the entire indictment in one sentence.**

The other thing that's true and nobody wants to say: the development pipeline behind the major-league roster is dry. The pitching prospects keep getting hurt. The position-player prospects keep getting traded for one-year rentals that don't work. **The team is not building anything.** It is patching, every year, in the same spot, with the same kind of player, with the same result.

I have watched this team beat itself in October for five straight Octobers. The first two, I blamed luck. The middle two, I blamed pitching match-ups. This one, if there even is an October for them, I am going to blame the roster build. Because the roster build is broken.

**Drop them.** They were top-5 in my preseason. They are not a top-10 team right now. The rankings move. The reason is on the page. Argue with me in the comments.

If you want a real top-3 team in baseball today, you have the **Dodgers**, the **Braves**, and the **Phillies**. The Yankees lost the hat-equals-contender game years ago. I just stopped pretending I still believed it.

— **Brad**
