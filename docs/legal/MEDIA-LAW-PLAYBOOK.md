# BB SPORTS MEDIA-LAW & COMPLIANCE PLAYBOOK — v1.0

> **This is not legal advice.** It is an operating playbook written to keep BB Sports out of trouble by default. Before public launch — and again before the first sponsorship dollar — have a Florida media lawyer spend one hour reviewing this document and the live site. Budget ~$300–500 for that hour. It is the cheapest insurance in this document.

---

## 0. THE TEN RULES (memorize these, everything else is detail)

1. **Being a free personal blog protects you from nothing.** "Non-commercial" is not a copyright defense, not a trademark defense, and defamation law doesn't care about revenue. The moment it's public, the law applies.
2. **You can always SAY a team's name. You can almost never USE a team's logo.** Words are yours. Marks are theirs.
3. **Never right-click-save an image.** Not from Google, not from X, not from Getty previews, not screenshots of broadcasts. Sports-photo copyright trolling is an industry; demand letters start at $750 per image.
4. **Facts are free, expression is not.** You can report any score, stat, trade, or injury in your own words. You cannot copy how someone else wrote it.
5. **Opinion is protected, false facts are not.** "The Yankees' front office is incompetent" is bulletproof. "The GM is on the take" is a lawsuit unless you can prove it.
6. **Quote a little, link a lot.** Short attributed quotes with a link to the source, always. Never reproduce someone's article, thread, or transcript wholesale.
7. **Embed only the original owner's post.** Official team/league/reporter accounts: fine. A random highlight account reposting broadcast clips: never — you inherit their infringement.
8. **Athletes' names in headlines: yes. Athletes' faces in anything that sells: no.** Editorial coverage is protected. Ads, merch, sponsored units, and promo graphics with a player's name/image/likeness are not.
9. **Every AI-touched thing is labeled, approved by Brad, and fact-checked.** A hallucinated stat published under Brad's byline is Brad's defamation problem, not the AI's.
10. **When money enters, the rules tighten.** First ad, first sponsor, first affiliate link → re-read §11 and §15 triggers. Several "OK for now" answers in this playbook flip.

## 1. OPERATING REALITY — what "personal blog, no revenue" does and doesn't mean

**Current posture (recorded 2026-07-15):** BB Sports is pre-launch, takes no revenue, sells nothing, and runs donations in interest-capture mode only.

What that reality actually buys you:

- **Lower damages exposure**, not lower liability. A rights-holder can still sue; they're just less motivated.
- **No FTC advertising obligations yet** (no sponsorships, no affiliate links, no endorsements). §11 activates the day that changes.
- **No sales-tax, business-license, or state privacy-law thresholds in play.**

What it does **not** buy you:

- **No fair-use free pass.** "Non-commercial" is one factor of four in fair use, and courts weight transformativeness and market harm far more. A free blog running a Getty photo loses the same way a paid one does.
- **No defamation slack.** Publication is publication. A student blogger with 40 readers can be sued exactly like ESPN.
- **No trademark slack.** Likelihood-of-confusion analysis doesn't ask whether you charged for it.

**Bottom line:** operate as if the site is commercial from day one, because the practices are identical and the site intends to become commercial.

## 3. COPYRIGHT — TEXT (reporting on other people's reporting)

This is a sports *takes* site that reacts to news broken by others (Schefter, Woj-style insiders, beat reporters). That model is legal **if expression is never copied**:

**Always allowed:**
- Reporting any **fact**: scores, stats, standings, trades, signings, injuries, quotes' substance. Facts are not copyrightable (*Feist*; *NBA v. Motorola* — scores/game facts are free to report).
- **Short quotes, attributed and linked.** Rule of thumb: ≤ 2 sentences from a written piece, always with the reporter's name and outlet, always linked (or embedded per §5).
- Writing Brad's own take on news someone else broke, **crediting the breaker**: "per Adam Schefter…" — this is both law-safe and journalism-ethics-correct.
- Quoting from press conferences, league statements, and press releases (issued to be quoted; still attribute).

**Never allowed:**
- Copying paragraphs, article structure, or "close paraphrase" (swapping synonyms into someone else's sentences).
- Republishing full tweets/threads as text walls (embed instead), full transcripts someone else produced, or another outlet's article "for reference."
- Systematic real-time lifting of one outlet's freshly reported scoops as a substitute for reading them ("hot news" misappropriation — mostly dead as a doctrine after *Barclays v. Theflyonthewall*, but the conduct also violates ToS and journalism ethics; don't be that site).

**The AI-specific version:** when the AI drafting pipeline (Perfection Engine Lane 5) summarizes source articles, the same rules bind the AI draft. The pipeline's system prompts must instruct: *facts out, expression rewritten, source named, link included.* Fact-check pass before Brad approves (Engine Component 12, case 22).

## 4. COPYRIGHT — IMAGES (the #1 way sports blogs actually get sued)

Founder delegated this policy ("you tell me"). **Policy set as follows — the Image Ladder.** Every image on BB Sports must come from one of these rungs, checked in order:

| Rung | Source | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AI-generated via the xAI media desk** (current pipeline) | Existing guardrails stand: no team logos, league marks, broadcast graphics, watermarks, player/celebrity likenesses (see `/docs/legal/XAI-GROK.md`). Credit line renders as configured. |
| 2 | **Brad's own photos** (games he attends, campus, himself) | Brad owns them. Watch for incidental crowd minors in close-up; prefer wide shots. Photos shot inside venues are copyright-fine (he's the author); ticket-back "no commercial photography" terms matter only if they're ever licensed out — for his own blog, low risk. |
| 3 | **Official embeds** (X posts, YouTube) for real game imagery | Per §5. The photo stays on the platform's servers; BB Sports never copies the file. |
| 4 | **Free-license stock** (Unsplash, Pexels) for generic texture — stadiums, footballs, crowds | Allowed with a license record: save image URL + license name + download date in the media ledger (`lib/media-assets.ts` metadata). **Do not** use identifiable athletes from stock even when the license allows — publicity rights (§7) are separate from copyright. |
| — | **NEVER** | Google Images, screenshots of broadcasts/streams, Getty/AP/Imagn images without a paid license, images ripped from X/Instagram, "found" logos, memes built on wire photos, watermark-cropped anything. |

**Why so strict:** sports photography enforcement is industrialized. Getty, agencies, and law firms (Higbee & Associates is the famous one) run reverse-image crawlers and send $750–$5,000 settlement demands to tiny blogs as a business model. Registered works carry statutory damages up to $30,000/work ($150,000 willful) plus fees. One saved-from-Google thumbnail can cost more than a year of hosting.

**Upgrade path (recorded for later):** when revenue supports it, a wire-photo subscription (Imagn is the budget entrant; AP/Getty editorial above it) unlocks real game photography under editorial license. Until then the AI-illustration aesthetic is a *brand strength* — lean into it.

**Admin enforcement:** the upload flow must require a source-rung selection + license fields before an image can attach to an article. (Engine Component 22 hard blocker "image source policy documented and enforced in admin upload flow" — this section is that policy.)

## 5. COPYRIGHT — VIDEO & EMBEDS (X, YouTube, highlights)

**The safe pattern BB Sports uses:** official platform embeds only, and **only of the original owner's own content.**

- **X embeds:** allowed for posts by the account that owns the content — teams, leagues, verified reporters posting their own reporting, athletes posting their own video. This is how every sports site quotes tweets.
- **YouTube embeds:** allowed for official channels — leagues, teams, broadcasters, press-conference hosts. Standard YouTube embed only; never download/re-upload, never clip and re-host.
- **The trap: infringing source posts.** Embedding a burner "highlights" account that reposted broadcast footage makes BB Sports part of that distribution chain. The embed defense (the "server test" — the file lives on the platform's servers, not ours) held in the Ninth Circuit (*Hunley v. Instagram*, 2023) but has been **rejected by courts elsewhere** (*Goldman v. Breitbart* (S.D.N.Y. 2018); W.D. Tex. 2025 in *UrbanImage v. iHeartMedia*), and Florida's Eleventh Circuit has no controlling answer. So: **the embed's legality is unsettled; the content's legitimacy is the control we own.** Embed rights-holders only.
- **Broadcast screenshots are copies, not embeds.** A frame-grab of a Fox/ESPN/TNT broadcast in an article or thumbnail is straightforward infringement. Never.
- **Deleted-post decay:** embeds die when the source deletes. Editorial rule: the article text must stand alone if every embed vanished.

## 6. TRADEMARK — TEAM NAMES, LOGOS, AND LEAGUE MARKS

**Founder decision (recorded 2026-07-15): Bradley selected "official logos via nominative fair use."** This section implements that decision as narrowly as the doctrine actually supports — and flags the honest risk.

**First, what needs no permission at all:**
- Using team, league, player, and event **names in text** — headlines, rankings, tags, analysis. "Chicago Bears," "NFL," "Stanley Cup Playoffs" in editorial copy is classic nominative use. This is why the current text + color-bug system is bulletproof.
- Brand-colored sport tags, as long as they don't imitate a specific logo's design.

**Nominative fair use — the real doctrine (from *New Kids on the Block v. News America*):** you may use another's mark when (1) the thing isn't identifiable without it, (2) you use **only as much as reasonably necessary**, and (3) you do **nothing that suggests sponsorship or endorsement**. Courts applying prong 2 have repeatedly said the *word* is usually necessary; the *logo* usually is not. That's the honest weakness in Bradley's chosen posture: a text site rarely *needs* the graphic.

**THE LOGO RULES (hard conditions — all eight must hold for any official logo to appear):**

1. **Editorial context only** — inside an article, ranking row, or scoreboard describing that team. Never decorative.
2. **Never in BB Sports' own identity** — no logos in the header, nav, favicon, app icon, default OG image, error pages, or the BB mark's vicinity. No composite lockups (BB monogram + team logo).
3. **Small and subordinate** — the logo never dominates the layout, never appears larger than BB Sports' own marks on the same surface, never as a full-bleed hero.
4. **Unaltered** — no recoloring, restyling, animating, or "BB Sports edition" remixes. (Alteration destroys the nominative defense and adds a derivative-work problem.)
5. **No commercial adjacency** — the moment ads or sponsorships ship, logos come off any page that carries a paid unit, and this decision is re-reviewed (§15 trigger). Logos never appear in anything sold, including merch, ever.
6. **Site-wide disclaimer** ships in the footer and /terms: *"BB Sports is an independent publication. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by the NFL, NHL, NBA, MLB, NCAA, or any league, conference, or team named on this site. Team names and marks are used for identification and commentary only."* **(Action item — not yet live.)**
7. **Protected event marks stay out of promo/commercial copy** — the NFL polices "Super Bowl," the NCAA polices "March Madness"/"Final Four," the USOPC polices "Olympics" with statutory superpowers (Ted Stevens Act). Reporting/commentary may name them; BB Sports promotions, sponsor packages, and merch may not ("the big game" convention exists for a reason).
8. **Provenance** — logo files come from a documented source (e.g., Wikimedia Commons vectors with source URL logged in the media ledger), not ripped from league apps in violation of their ToS.

**Honest risk statement:** leagues send cease-and-desists to independent sites over logo walls and thumbnails regardless of the merits, because they can. Even a winnable nominative-fair-use fight costs five figures. The **safer default remains text + color only** (the current build), and nothing in the product *requires* logos. **Recommendation: keep the current logo-free build; treat the Logo Rules as the ceiling if/when a surface truly needs a mark. Re-confirm with counsel before the first logo ships.**

**Decision status:** FOUNDER-SELECTED (fair use with conditions) / OPERATOR-RECOMMENDED (stay text-only). The stricter of the two governs until counsel reviews.

## 7. RIGHT OF PUBLICITY — PLAYERS, COACHES, AND NIL

Separate from copyright and trademark: people own commercial use of their **name, image, and likeness**. Florida codifies this (Fla. Stat. §540.08) with an express **newsworthiness exception** — and sports commentary is squarely newsworthy.

**Protected (no consent needed):**
- Writing about any athlete, coach, executive: news, analysis, criticism, rankings, headlines with their names.
- Brad's own photos of public game action used editorially.
- Official embeds of their content.

**Prohibited without a signed license:**
- Any athlete's name/image/likeness in **advertising, sponsorship units, or anything implying endorsement** ("Player X reads BB Sports").
- **Merch** with player names, numbers + team color combos, faces, or nicknames. (Jersey-affiliate links to licensed retailers are fine — that's the retailer's license.)
- **AI-generated player or celebrity likenesses** — already banned in the xAI prompt guardrails; reaffirmed here as a publicity-rights rule, not just a style rule.
- Quote graphics styled to look like the athlete produced or endorsed them for BB Sports promo use. (Sharing a real, attributed quote inside editorial content: fine.)

**Extra care classes (Engine already flags these):** minors in recruiting/NIL coverage — names only in the context of their public sports activity, no personal contact info, no monetized features built on a minor's identity.

## 8. DEFAMATION — THE ACTUAL BIGGEST RISK FOR A TAKES SITE

Copyright trolls want money; defamation plaintiffs want blood. For an opinion-led site, this is the section that protects Bradley's career.

**The doctrine in four lines:**
- **Pure opinion is protected.** "The Lakers are not a real team" cannot be false; it's a viewpoint.
- **Opinion implying undisclosed false facts is NOT protected** (*Milkovich v. Lorain Journal*). "Something's fishy about how that contract got done" implies facts.
- **Public figures** (players, coaches, GMs, owners, agents — nearly everyone BB Sports covers) must prove **actual malice**: Brad knew it was false or recklessly ignored the truth. High bar — but "I saw it in a random tweet and ran it" flirts with reckless.
- **Rhetorical hyperbole is protected.** Obvious exaggeration nobody takes literally ("the front office is asleep," "that trade was a crime against the fanbase") is safe *because* it's obviously not factual.

**THE PRE-PUBLISH CHECKLIST (runs on every article — human or AI-drafted):**

1. Every **factual claim** is sourced — linked, attributed, or first-hand. No load-bearing facts from anonymous burner accounts.
2. Opinions are **built on disclosed facts**: state the facts (sourced), then the take. "He's 2-for-19 in one-score games *(link)* — that's a coach who shrinks" is the protected pattern.
3. **Red-flag accusations get elevated scrutiny** — claims of crime, PED/cheating, match-fixing, addiction, sexual misconduct, or professional corruption are defamation-per-se territory. Rule: two independent reputable sources or the person's own statement, or it doesn't ship. When in doubt: frame as the reported allegation it is ("per the AP, prosecutors allege…"), never as established fact.
4. **Quotes are verbatim and sourced.** No reconstructed quotes, no "basically said." (Engine hard limit already.)
5. **Headlines can't check a claim the body doesn't cash.** The headline is the most-sued sentence on any page.
6. Satire/bits are **unmistakably** satire.
7. AI drafts get a **hallucination pass**: every stat, date, and quote in an AI draft is verified against a source before Brad's approve click (a fabricated stat about a real person is a defamation vector, not a typo).

**If a demand or mistake surfaces — Florida's retraction lane:** Fla. Stat. §770.01–.02 gives media defendants (courts have extended this to bloggers, e.g. *Comins v. VanVoorhis*) real protection when a **full, fair, prompt correction** is published — it can cut plaintiffs to actual damages only. BB Sports already has `/corrections`. Operating rule: any substantiated factual error is corrected **within 24 hours**, dated, linked from the corrected article (Engine Component 12, case 4) — that habit is also a legal damages shield. Any legal-sounding demand letter goes to a lawyer *before* replying; never argue by email.

**Also:** Florida's anti-SLAPP statute (§768.295) provides some protection against suits meant to silence protected speech — another reason meritless threats usually die at the letter stage when your process is clean.

## 9. COMMENTS & UGC — SECTION 230 AND THE DMCA SAFE HARBOR

BB Sports runs first-party, Reddit-style comments. Two shields cover user content, and one requires action:

**Shield 1 — Section 230 (47 U.S.C. §230):** BB Sports is not the publisher of what commenters write. Defamation in a comment is the commenter's problem, not Brad's. Crucially, **moderating does not waive this** — delete, filter, and ban freely ("Good Samaritan" clause). Two ways to lose it: (a) materially **editing a comment's meaning** (fixing typos fine; changing "I heard X" to "X is true" makes it yours), or (b) writing the content yourself (Brad's replies are his own speech, fully attributable to him).

**Shield 2 — DMCA safe harbor (17 U.S.C. §512) for copyrighted material users post:** Section 230 explicitly does **not** cover IP claims. If a commenter pastes a copyrighted photo/article, safe harbor is what protects the site — and it **only applies if the site registers a designated agent with the U.S. Copyright Office.** Verified July 2026: **$6 online registration, ~10 minutes, renew every 3 years** (calendar the renewal — an expired designation forfeits the harbor).

**ACTION ITEMS (gap found in live audit, 2026-07-15):**
1. **Register the DMCA designated agent** at dmca.copyright.gov ($6). Blocker-level before comments open publicly.
2. **Update `/dmca`** to display the agent's name/address/email exactly as registered (currently it routes to /contact — insufficient for §512(c)(2)'s "available on the website" requirement).
3. **Adopt and publish a repeat-infringer policy** (required by §512(i)): e.g., "accounts/IPs with two substantiated infringement takedowns are banned" — add to /dmca and enforce via the moderation queue.
4. **Takedown workflow:** valid notice → remove promptly → log → forward counter-notice procedure. The existing moderation queue is the natural home.

**Comment-box hygiene already handled by the Engine:** rate limits, spam filtering, no mock engagement, evidence preservation during brigades.

## 11. MONEY RULES — donations now; ads, sponsors, affiliate later

Two standing rules: never describe donations in ways that imply charity status, and never let a donor's status touch coverage (already in terms).

**On first sponsorship / ad / affiliate dollar (FTC — 2023 Endorsement Guides, verified current):**
- Every sponsored piece carries a **clear and conspicuous** disclosure **in the content itself** — top of the article, not a footer hashtag. "Sponsored by X" / "X paid for this placement." Vague labels ("partner," "collab," "spon") fail the standard.
- **Material connections are disclosed regardless of form** — free gear, comp'd tickets, or affiliate commissions count the same as cash. Jersey-affiliate links (the one approved affiliate category) get "BB Sports earns a commission on this link" adjacent to the link.
- Editorial/sponsored separation is visible: sponsors never buy rankings positions, takes, or silence (already in donation terms; extend verbatim to the sponsorship kit).
- **No gambling promotion, ever** (Engine hard limit; also sidesteps state-by-state gambling-ad law entirely). Betting lines may be cited *as data* in analysis.
- Comp'd press access or review products, if ever accepted, are disclosed in-piece.

## 12. SPORTS DATA, SCORES, AND STATS

- **Facts are free** (*NBA v. Motorola*): anyone may report scores, schedules, stats, and standings in their own presentation.
- **Feeds are contracts:** the way you *obtain* data is governed by ToS even when the data itself is free. No scraping ESPN/league apps/Stats Perform properties against their terms; no free-tier API keys ("non-commercial use") in production — that's exactly the trap the Engine's `BBSPORTS_APPROVED_LIVE_SCORES` gate exists for. Keep the gate. When v1.2's live-scores ship approaches, license a commercial feed (SportsDataIO / Sportradar / API-Sports commercial tier) and store the terms in this folder.
- **Freshness labeling** (Engine rule): any scores/standings surface shows its as-of timestamp. That's a trust rule, but it's also a warranty-ish honesty rule — stale data presented as live is the kind of thing /terms disclaims and we shouldn't need to lean on.
- **Betting data:** lines/movement citable as facts within analysis; never packaged as picks (§11).

## 13. AI-SPECIFIC LAYER

The Engine already enforces the big four (label everything AI-touched, Brad approves everything, AI never mimics Brad, ≤300-word AI drafts). The legal notes on top:

- **Ownership:** pure AI output has no copyright (US Copyright Office human-authorship rule). Practical impact: near zero — Brad's edits, arrangement, and the "Bradley's Take" additions create protectable human authorship, and nobody's business model here is licensing article text. Don't sweat it; do keep Brad's editing pass real.
- **Liability flows to the publisher.** "The AI wrote it" is not a defense to defamation or infringement — Brad's approve click is the point of responsibility. That's why §8's hallucination pass is mandatory, not stylistic.
- **Training/input hygiene:** don't paste other outlets' full articles into prompts as drafting material (that's §3's copying rule at the input stage). Summaries, facts, and links in; original expression out.
- **Provider terms:** xAI usage terms + the logo/likeness prompt bans live in `XAI-GROK.md` and remain binding. The image-generation guardrails double as §6/§7 compliance.
- **Disclosure honesty runs both directions** (Engine rule): never label human work as AI, never pass AI work as purely human. The existing "AI-assisted, Brad-edited" badge is the standard.
